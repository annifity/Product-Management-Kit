# Product Strategy And Portfolio Workflow

Use when the decision spans product direction, multiple opportunities, or investment choices rather than the sequencing of an already confirmed delivery scope.

## Decision Contract

- Name the decision owner, decision horizon, affected products or initiatives, and review date.
- Resolve the current strategic baseline and accepted commitments before recommending change.
- Separate evidence, inference, assumption, constraint, and preference.
- Treat strategy as a set of choices and exclusions, not a list of desirable outcomes.

## Workflow

1. Frame the strategic problem, target users or segments, outcome, constraints, and horizon.
2. Establish evidence quality, current performance, committed work, available capacity, and material dependencies.
3. Define coherent alternatives, including maintain or stop; state where each option plays, how it wins, and what it excludes.
4. Compare alternatives using outcome contribution, confidence, strategic fit, economics, risk, reversibility, dependency, and time to evidence.
5. For a portfolio, classify each bet as explore, validate, invest, sustain, sequence, pause, or stop. Do not force one scoring formula across unlike horizons.
6. Select the minimum coherent set of bets that fits constraints. Record displaced work and opportunity cost.
7. Define leading and lagging outcome signals, guardrails, invalidation conditions, and review triggers.
8. Capture the decision, rationale, dissent, owner, decision date, review date, and downstream gates.

## Boundaries

- `discovery` owns unresolved opportunity framing and raw research.
- `learn` owns interpretation of assessed evidence.
- `strategy` owns product and portfolio choices, exclusions, and investment posture.
- `spec` owns detailed delivery behavior.
- `plan` owns release slicing, dependencies, milestones, and sequence for stable scope.
- A strategic roadmap communicates outcomes, themes, and bets; it must not silently promise dates or implementation scope.

## AI-Specific Decisions

When AI is material, include:

- why AI is more suitable than a deterministic or operational alternative;
- minimum useful autonomy and human authority;
- risk tier and evidence burden;
- data, evaluation, monitoring, and unit-economics implications;
- capability build/buy/partner choice;
- failure, fallback, and exit posture.

## Exit Gate

A strategy decision is ready to hand off only when its owner, horizon, choices, exclusions, outcome measures, evidence confidence, constraints, affected commitments, and review triggers are explicit.
