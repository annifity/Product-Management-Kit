# Go-To-Market And Adoption Workflow

Use when a product change needs coordinated positioning, audience targeting, enablement, launch channels, adoption measurement, and post-launch adjustment. This workflow does not replace release safety or product strategy.

## Input Contract

- Accepted product strategy or launch intent.
- Confirmed release scope, target users or segments, value proposition, availability, constraints, and owner.
- Evidence for claims and a clear distinction between committed behavior and planned messaging.
- Release readiness and rollback remain owned by `ship`.

## Workflow

1. Define the target segment, job or problem, value proposition, differentiation, and desired behavior change.
2. Separate launch audiences: buyer, user, approver, administrator, support, sales, operations, and affected non-users.
3. Define positioning and evidence-backed claims. Do not promise roadmap items or unsupported AI quality.
4. Select launch motion, channel, timing, eligibility, pricing or packaging implication, and regional or policy constraints.
5. Prepare onboarding, migration, enablement, support, sales, partner, and internal communication needs.
6. Define adoption funnel, activation event, time-to-value, retention signal, guardrails, segment thresholds, and instrumentation.
7. Connect launch stages to release controls: pilot, limited availability, general availability, pause, rollback, and support escalation.
8. Assign owners and decision dates for launch readiness, adoption review, corrective action, and learning capture.

## Output

- Audience and positioning
- Evidence-backed value proposition and claims
- Launch motion, channel, timing, and eligibility
- Onboarding, enablement, migration, and support plan
- Stakeholder communication matrix
- Adoption funnel, measures, thresholds, and guardrails
- Rollout dependencies, risks, and owners
- Post-launch review cadence and decisions

## Boundaries

- `strategy` owns target segment, positioning choice, and investment posture.
- `ship` owns release readiness, rollout safety, rollback, support, and external-action approval.
- `validate` owns the evidence-quality verdict.
- `learn` owns post-launch interpretation and the next product decision.
